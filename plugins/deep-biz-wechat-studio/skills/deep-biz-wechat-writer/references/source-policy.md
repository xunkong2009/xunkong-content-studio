# Source Policy

## Goal

Build articles on evidence sturdy enough to support interpretation, not just information density.

## Priority Order

1. Primary material: earnings calls, investor letters, SEC or exchange filings, policy texts, official statistics, transcripts, direct interviews, original datasets.
2. High-trust reporting: Bloomberg, Reuters, Financial Times, The Economist, New York Times, Businessweek, WSJ, Caixin, 36Kr, Huxiu, LatePost, Fast Company, Adweek.
3. Strong secondary signal: Business Insider, Medium essays by domain experts, quality Substack newsletters, credible trade media.
4. Community signal only: Reddit, forums, social posts. Use for color, sentiment, and pattern detection, not as sole proof.

## Cross-Verification Rules

- Any article-driving claim should be confirmed by at least two independent sources when possible.
- If only one strong source exists, say so.
- If sources disagree, preserve the disagreement and explain what is still uncertain.
- Separate "reported", "confirmed", and "inferred".

## Fact Versus Inference

- Fact: directly supported by a cited source or first-party material.
- Inference: reasoned conclusion drawn from multiple facts, behavior patterns, or market context.
- Speculation: plausible but weakly evidenced forecast. Use sparingly and label it clearly.

## Time Sensitivity

- For current topics, verify publication dates and event dates before writing.
- Prefer the most recent trustworthy source, but do not sacrifice rigor for freshness.
- When the user says "最近", "今天", or "最新", verify the actual date explicitly.

## Research Coverage

- Cover Chinese and global sources when the story crosses markets, technology stacks, or consumer culture.
- For topic scanning, default to a mixed source pool even before a topic is chosen. Do not rely on domestic heat alone if strong global reporting reveals a more important shift.
- The preferred global scan pool includes Bloomberg, Reuters, Financial Times, The Wall Street Journal, The New York Times, The Economist, Bloomberg Businessweek, Business Insider, Fast Company, Adweek, strong Medium essays, and high-quality Substack newsletters.
- For brand and marketing topics, look for campaign intent, distribution channel, audience reaction, conversion logic, and business outcome.
- For technology topics, look for product capability, monetization pressure, market structure, and adoption friction.

## Use Of Lower-Tier Sources

- Use Reddit and community discussion to discover hypotheses, user sentiment, or product pain points.
- Never use anecdotal community material as the only support for a large business claim.
- If a weaker source contributes a useful angle, pair it with a stronger reporting or primary source.

## Citation Behavior

- Name the source clearly in the drafting materials.
- In the final article, integrate sourcing naturally instead of turning the piece into a bibliography dump.
- Keep quotes short and only when the exact wording matters.

## Cultural Works And Lived Scenes

- Cultural material includes books, films, television, essays, poetry, songs, games, art and advertising. For wording, concepts, plots and historical particulars, consult original works, reliable editions, official excerpts/transcripts, or clearly attributed authoritative discussions. Record version, chapter, scene or a usable source location when available; never invent a page or timestamp.
- Reading an author's old article verifies how the author wrote, not the accuracy of the article's quotations, plot attributions, statistics or causal claims. Recheck material before using it as fact in a new piece.
- Distinguish exact quotation, faithful paraphrase, interpretive analogy and explicitly hypothetical extension. Removing quotation marks does not remove the need to verify attribution and meaning. If the source cannot be established, omit or replace the material; do not camouflage it with a vague attribution.
- A fictional scene may illuminate a human predicament; it cannot prove real market prevalence. A theory may offer a lens without proving that every consumer follows it. A work claimed to have caused a business outcome needs evidence beyond resemblance or familiarity.
- For scenes, distinguish the user's documented experience, a reported event, a general illustration and a stated hypothetical. Preserve the original time and scope of old first-person experiences. Do not invent personal encounters, friends, dialogue or biography to create authorial presence.
- Follow the final-draft punctuation preferences without silently altering exact quotes or official work titles. Prefer clearly attributed paraphrase when exact wording is unnecessary; honor an explicit user request for faithful quotation. Keep quotations brief and respect applicable source and copyright limits.
