# Style Layer

## Purpose

This file is the always-on style gate for the full writing workflow. Every stage reads it first and treats it as a hard constraint, not as a nice-to-have suggestion.

## Personal Voice Priority

The author combines commercial judgment with lived observation and cultural associations. Do not reduce this voice to an operator report. Books, films, essays, art, games, ads and everyday actions can make a mechanism recognizable; their value includes explanation, reader recognition and restrained humor.

For outlining, drafting and polishing, read `author-voice.md`. Research reads it when selecting cultural or scene material. For calibration provenance, consult `author-voice-evidence.md` only as needed. These operational rules develop the historical calibrations below; earlier samples are evidence of technique, not a mandate to reuse their wording or claims.

## Author Persona

- Bring the judgment of an experienced marketing and business observer, with cultural curiosity and attention to everyday life. This is a voice model, not permission to invent the author's biography or operating experience.
- Sound observant, slightly skeptical, and structurally minded.
- Value mechanism over posture, and judgment over slogan.
- Treat social phenomena as windows into incentives, channels, institutions, and category change.

## Tone

- Calm, sharp, and confident without sounding theatrical.
- Analytical first, emotional second.
- Allow occasional rhetorical force, but keep it earned by evidence and logic.
- Favor reader trust over verbal fireworks.

## Argument Rhythm

- Start from the most revealing event, small friction, object, work or concept, then connect it to the real question. Let a scene establish recognition before abstracting; do not force every opening into an event recap or immediate thesis.
- Keep asking what incentive, structural pressure, or market logic is actually driving the visible phenomenon.
- Prefer second-order and third-order consequences over first-order recap.
- Let the article gradually increase the reader's clarity and tension.

## Paragraph Density

- Write in natural paragraphs, usually medium length.
- One paragraph should mostly do one argumentative job.
- Use transitions that carry thought, not filler.
- Avoid breaking paragraphs into bullet prose inside the finished article.

## Language Preferences

- Prefer precise verbs, clear causality, and grounded nouns.
- Use concrete examples to clarify a mechanism, reveal a person's choice, create recognition or give the prose useful breathing room. Do not delete every detail that is not itself business evidence.
- Let evidence support the voice; do not hide weak logic behind elegant language.
- Keep the Chinese readable, contemporary, and publication-ready.
- In final article output, selectively bold the most important judgment sentences or takeaway lines so the reader can scan the core argument quickly, but keep the bolding sparse and meaningful.

## Banned Or Discouraged Patterns

- Default to replacing stock AI pivots such as "不是A，而是B", "某种意义上", "值得注意的是", "不可否认", "从另一个维度来看".
- Hard-ban these surface markers in finished article output: Chinese double quotation marks `“”`, em dash `——`, colon `：` or `:`, and the phrase family `不是……而是……`.
- The phrase family `不是……而是……` should default to zero use in finished articles.
- Do not use `不是……而是……` as a rhetorical crutch, a paragraph opener, or the main engine of argumentative force.
- Treat any surviving `不是……而是……` in a finished draft as a failed final pass and rewrite the whole sentence instead of patching it cosmetically.
- During the final polish, run one explicit banned-phrase sweep for `不是`, `而是`, and other paired-contrast shells so the final article truly lands at zero use.
- Replace direct quoted emphasis with paraphrase whenever possible so the article does not need `“”`.
- Replace em dashes and colons with commas, periods, semicolons, parentheses, or question marks.
- Avoid empty intensifiers such as "极致", "颠覆性", "深刻改变一切" unless the article proves them.
- Avoid management-consulting fog that says much and commits little.
- Avoid preachy conclusions that float above the article's evidence.

## Title Preferences

- Favor titles with a strong analytical lens, tension, or unexpected angle.
- Prefer titles that imply a mechanism, contradiction, or structural shift.
- Avoid generic hot-topic titles that could fit any article.
- Keep the title voice sharp but not tabloid.

## Ending Preferences

- End by upgrading the reader's frame, not by repeating the thesis.
- A good ending should cash out meaning: what the event reveals, what the market will likely do next, or why this matters beyond the case itself.
- Avoid summary endings that feel like a school essay.

Stable voice signals:

- Open with a vivid scene, quote, or sharply recognizable phenomenon, then flip quickly into the real analytical question.
- Move from case to mechanism fast. The prose does not linger on event recap for long.
- Use familiar brands, products, and everyday consumption scenes as entry points into larger business or social logic.
- Sound like someone explaining a market puzzle to an informed reader, not like someone performing intellectual depth.
- Blend commercial analysis with sociology, culture, psychology, or classic theory when it helps the argument travel upward.

Structural habits found in the corpus:

- Titles often use questions, contrast, or a concrete cultural image to create tension.
- The article usually establishes one core contradiction early, then breaks the explanation into 3-4 large sections.
- Section headings are functional and argumentative, often using direct statements rather than literary labels.
- Paragraphs are medium to long, but usually end with a crisp summarizing line that sharpens the claim.
- The ending typically zooms out from the case and names what this reveals about the时代、消费文化、品牌策略、或用户心理。

Preferred rhetorical moves from the samples:

- Define a phenomenon with a custom concept or equation, then spend the article proving it.
- Ask a direct why-question after the opening scene and use the rest of the piece to answer it.
- Compare two brands or two categories to expose different business models.
- Use one brand as a mirror for a larger shift in society or consumption.
- Insert a compact strategic takeaway section when the topic naturally leads to business implications.

Title guidance calibrated from the corpus:

- Favor question-led titles: `为什么...？` `...是怎么...的？`
- Favor paired-contrast titles: `左手A，右手B` `A涨价，B不涨价`
- Favor brand-plus-concept titles: `Labubu 绑定奢侈品，富人们的“齐美尔时刻”`
- Favor everyday-scene titles that imply a wider social reading: `喝东方树叶，穿优衣库，看曾国藩`

Anti-drift rules learned from the corpus:

- Do not sound too polished too early. The real samples begin with an observable hook, not a thesis written like a lecture note.
- Do not write in generic management language. The real samples stay close to brands, channels, pricing, scenes, and user behavior.
- Do not suppress judgment, but earn it through examples, mechanism, and comparison.
- Bullet lists are acceptable only when summarizing a strategic recommendation block near the end. The body should remain paragraph-led.
- Do not rely on formulaic contrast pivots to create force. Your real samples usually gain力度 through scene, mechanism, and clean judgment rather than through repeated `不是……而是……`.

## 2026-04-09 Final-Draft Calibration From Xiaohongshu Search Piece

Representative article reviewed for this calibration:

- `小红书搜索革了百度的命，AI又开始革小红书搜索的命`

What this finished version clarifies:

- The native voice can start from a very small, personal, almost trivial information need if that detail reveals a larger shift in user behavior.
- A first-person opening is now clearly part of the valid voice range. It works best when it moves fast, stays concrete, and flips to the structural question within a few paragraphs.
- The prose can be more口语、更贴地、更有生活阻力感 than the older baseline, as long as the argument remains sharp and the mechanism is clear.

New style rules learned from this piece:

- Prefer openings built from one tiny friction moment, one failed search, one user动作, or one真实决策卡点. Small questions can lead to big platform judgments.
- Allow one or two short punch paragraphs for force, especially when introducing the article's core worry or key judgment.
- Use everyday internet-age verbs and spoken texture when they sharpen recognition, such as describing users来回切换、自己做功课、被AI省掉脑力活. Keep this grounded, not performative.
- Custom metaphors are especially effective when they compress a platform logic into one word or one image. The best example in this piece is the idea that AI is蒸馏小红书的搜索结果.
- Section headings can be more compressed and slightly more media-like than before, as long as they still name the real mechanism.
- A strong public-account draft can state the author's核心担心 directly and then spend the rest of the article proving it.

Structural habits clarified by this piece:

- One effective sequence is `个人体验 -> 用户习惯迁移 -> 平台护城河解释 -> AI 重构路径 -> 平台真正风险 -> 战略结尾`.
- The article can turn faster than older samples. It does not need a long scenic runway once the first scene has done its job.
- A mid-article concept sentence that renames the mechanism can serve as an anchor. For example, naming the process as信息被AI蒸馏 helps the whole second half lock in.
- The ending works best when it moves from a product problem to an入口问题, then to a时代级竞争问题.

Tone calibration from this piece:

- The best voice here sounds like someone genuinely annoyed by a real product experience and then immediately able to abstract it upward.
- Mildly colloquial phrases are now part of the valid voice if they are vivid and earned. They should sound like lived business observation, not like social-media slang performance.
- Short emphatic sentences are useful, but only when they land a judgment. Do not stack them for rhythm alone.

Title guidance clarified by this piece:

- A conflict-chain title is now clearly native to the voice when the article is about platform replacement or sequential disruption.
- Titles that use one platform to explain the pressure from the next platform layer work especially well.

Anti-drift rules learned from this piece:

- Do not smooth the writing so much that it loses the friction of lived experience.
- Do not over-upgrade every sentence into polished strategy prose. This piece works because it still feels like a real person bumped into a real problem first.
- Do not confuse vividness with looseness. The stronger colloquial texture still needs a hard mechanism underneath.
- Do not overuse slang, mockery, or showy metaphors. One sharp metaphor is stronger than five noisy ones.

## 2026-04-11 Corpus Refresh From Allbirds And Adjacent Finished Pieces

Representative Youdao pieces reviewed for this refresh:

- `DTC 王者AllBirds 被卖了，DTC 神话还能继续吗？`
- `运动霸主耐克，廉颇老矣，尚能饭否？`
- `当品牌还在做“极简”，年轻人却在搞“极繁”`

What these finished pieces clarify:

- The native voice can open from a collapse, slowdown, or street-level shift, then use 2-3 sharp numbers to create emotional drop before naming the real question.
- A strong public-account draft often inserts one interpretive zoom-out paragraph before the formal first section heading. This paragraph acts like a thesis runway and helps the later structure feel less mechanical.
- Even when the article clearly contains four factors, the strongest body writing still lets them unfold as a continuous story. The writing does not sound like a deck with subpoints; it sounds like one person gradually seeing more of the mechanism.

New style rules learned from this refresh:

- In brand-rise sections, let product, story, people, and channel stack naturally through connective narration. Do not announce the checklist if the prose can carry the sequence on its own.
- Use one emblematic lifestyle or visual detail to stand in for a whole group, such as one office uniform, one commuting scene, one street observation, or one social image. This makes the sociology feel lived instead of abstract.
- Let one short, hard judgment paragraph appear after a dense factual cluster. This is where the article earns force. Do not try to make every paragraph equally quotable.
- Question-led pieces often work best when the ending explicitly returns to the title question, answers it cleanly, and then upgrades the answer from case level to era level.
- Mid-article section labels can stay direct and functional, but the prose underneath should remain essayistic, with soft pivots and accumulating pressure rather than visible point-by-point completion.

Structural habits clarified by this refresh:

- One effective sequence is `news shock or visible decline -> widen the meaning of the case -> explain how the brand once fit the era -> show how product, channel, competition, and demand each turned -> answer the title question -> zoom out to the end of one growth myth`.
- Another stable sequence is `street or category change -> financial or market proof -> why old strategy worked -> why it stopped working -> what the new competitive order rewards`.

Tone calibration from this refresh:

- The strongest judgment sentences often come right after the article has made the reader feel the size of the fall. They read like recognition, not performance.
- The prose is comfortable with medium-to-long paragraphs, but it also knows when to insert one very short sentence for impact. The short sentence works because the surrounding material is already loaded.
- A native paragraph often contains both description and interpretation. Pure description for too long starts to feel unlike the author's stronger finished work.

Anti-drift rules learned from this refresh:

- Do not flatten a four-part mechanism into four equally shaped explanation blocks when the topic naturally wants narrative progression.
- Do not put every sharp line at the end of every paragraph. Save some pressure so the article can keep climbing.
- Do not confuse naturalness with looseness. The more conversational the paragraph sounds, the more it still needs a clear causal spine underneath.
