# Source Policy

## Goal

Build NEV industry articles on evidence sturdy enough to support interpretation, not just information density.

## Priority Order

1. Primary material: earnings calls, investor letters, annual or quarterly reports, exchange filings, prospectuses, policy texts, official statistics, direct interviews, original datasets, product launches, and technical white papers.
2. High-priority industry sources: 工信部, 乘联会, 中汽协, 海关总署, 国家统计局, 各地监管与补贴政策原文.
3. High-trust reporting: Reuters, Bloomberg, Financial Times, The Economist, WSJ, Caixin, LatePost, 36Kr, Huxiu, Jiemian, Yicai, Businessweek.
4. Strong secondary signal: quality trade media, supplier research, expert newsletters, sector notes, and long-form interviews.
5. Community signal only: forums, social posts, owner communities, and public discussion. Use for sentiment and hypothesis generation, never as sole proof.

## Cross-Verification Rules

- Any article-driving claim should be confirmed by at least two independent sources when possible.
- If only one strong source exists, say so.
- If sources disagree, preserve the disagreement and explain what is still uncertain.
- Separate `reported`, `confirmed`, and `inferred`.

## Fact Versus Inference

- Fact: directly supported by a cited source or first-party material.
- Inference: a reasoned conclusion drawn from multiple facts, company behavior, or industry context.
- Speculation: a plausible but weakly evidenced forecast. Use sparingly and label it clearly.

## Time Sensitivity

- For current topics, verify publication dates and event dates before writing.
- Prefer the most recent trustworthy source, but do not sacrifice rigor for freshness.
- When the user says "最近", "今天", or "最新", verify the actual date explicitly.

## Required Citation Behavior

- Any key statement about 车型参数, 销量, 交付, 盈利, 毛利率, 电池技术, 补能能力, 智驾能力, or 海外布局 should carry a clear source name and date in the research stage.
- If a technical capability depends on region, trim, testing standard, or software version, say so.
- If a number comes from management guidance rather than delivered fact, label it as guidance.

## Research Coverage

- For company topics, cover product mix, price band, gross margin trend, cost structure, cash position, and next-stage competitive pressure.
- For technology topics, cover capability boundary, hardware stack, software maturity, cost, monetization pressure, and adoption friction.
- For battery and supply-chain topics, cover chemistry route, capacity, customer concentration, bargaining power, and policy exposure.
- For charging or swapping topics, cover infrastructure economics, user convenience, capital intensity, and standardization risk.
- For overseas expansion topics, cover tariffs, local production, channel setup, localization, and political risk.

## Use Of Lower-Tier Sources

- Use community discussion to discover hypotheses, product pain points, or owner sentiment.
- Never use anecdotal community material as the only support for a large industry claim.
- Pair weaker sources with stronger reporting or primary material whenever they contribute a useful angle.

## Citation Behavior In Drafting

- Name the source clearly in drafting materials.
- In the final article, integrate sourcing naturally instead of turning the piece into a bibliography dump.
- Keep quotes short and only when the exact wording matters.
